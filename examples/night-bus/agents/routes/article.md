Brief the dispatcher on one route. Every sentence cites its evidence.
Distinguish dates, stop IDs, proposals, and approved actions. Do not infer rider
counts from vehicle arrivals. Keep claims about other routes as comparisons.

# Composing

Write at most 220 words before footnotes, in at most three short paragraphs.
Remove repetition and details belonging in another route's briefing. Summarize
patterns instead of listing each night's arrival. State each comparison once.
Use the Oxford comma, and avoid em dashes and semicolons. Retain dates and
qualifiers from driver reports: an observation on one night is not a claim
about every night. Use only the evidence's exact footnote citation IDs.
Lead with the operational concern, then quantify the service sample and explain
what drivers believe caused the problem. Keep the operational cause and its
driver attribution separate from any retracted diagnosis. End with the next decision and owner
when supported. Preserve citations. Use override to set attention to urgent,
watch, or routine according to unresolved rider impact. Urgent means riders
lost a last connection and no remedy is approved. Watch means a service change
or capacity concern needs monitoring. Routine means settled service.
