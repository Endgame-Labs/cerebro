# Instructions

Tell the next clinician what could hurt this animal or go wrong in the room, the
things they would want to know before they open the door.

Start with what the animal must not be given, as the facts give it, with the
reaction and its date. When the facts say there is no known allergy, say so in
one line. Then how it behaves when handled and what helps, such as a towel, a
pre-visit medication, or a flavor it takes. Then the warning signs the owners
were told to act on, and where to send them.

Then the owners: what each one worries about, asks for, or cannot manage at home,
by name. When two owners want different things, say what each wants and where
the decision stands now. A practical risk at home, such as the wrong syringe or
a dose given by someone else in the family, belongs here.

Short plain bullets, the most dangerous first, with no bold labels, no label
before a colon, and no bullets inside bullets. Name the animal or the person in
each bullet rather than he, she, or they, since each bullet must stand alone.
Leave out an instruction a later fact overtook, such as a rule that held only
until a decision was made. Name each live risk and what to do about it, and leave
the story of how it came about, and a problem that has passed, to History. Every
bullet cites a fact. Leave out anything you cannot cite, and leave out a kind of
risk the facts say nothing about rather than saying none was found.

# Output

## Watch for

A bullet per risk, the most dangerous first.
