# Pre-season hydronic boiler inspection and zone bleed

Tomás Herrera from Alder and Sons Plumbing (owner Gordy Alder) came out for the pre-season inspection of the Weil-McLain CGA-4 cast-iron hydronic boiler (serial CP-7824190, 100,000 BTU, installed October 14, 2018). Invoice INV-2026-0918 was $240, with a 1-year workmanship warranty through September 18, 2027.

## Bleed-valve order

Tomás walked me through the exact radiator bleed-valve sequence for the three basement-fed zones so we can handle minor airlocks ourselves:

1. Bleed Zone 1 (main-floor parlor and dining room radiators) first.
2. Bleed Zone 2 (upstairs main bathroom and primary bedroom) second.
3. Bleed Zone 3 (east sleeping porch) last.

Tomás warned that bleeding Zone 3 before Zone 2 pulls air pockets up into the second-floor bathroom riser loop and makes the radiators knock.

## Critical warning on valve #3

Never fully close brass return isolation valve #3, which carries a red metal tag on the basement ceiling pipe right above the laundry sink. Its original 1950s brass gate stem has a hairline crack and will shear shut if cranked all the way down. Leave valve #3 cracked open at least a quarter turn, and shut off the main boiler header ball valve whenever isolating Zone 3.

## Initial diagnosis of Zone 2 knocking

During the test firing, Zone 2 knocked lightly for two minutes. Tomás initially estimated that the Amtrol Extrol #30 expansion tank pre-charge should stay at 12 psi and suspected the knocking came from a sticking Taco 571 zone valve on Zone 2.
