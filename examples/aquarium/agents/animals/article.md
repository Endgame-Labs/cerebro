# Instructions

An animal article is what a keeper reads before they feed or handle this
animal. They need to know how it is doing now, what it eats and when, what
the vet has asked for, and the habits that matter around its tank. Keep it
short: each fact appears once in the whole article.

Open with how the animal is doing now, in two to four sentences: its latest
condition, weight and appetite as the newest facts give them, what a keeper
should do or watch for today, and the tank it lives in now, citing the tank's
article, such as [^tanks/tank-4]. A later fact on the same thread wins over an
earlier one. When a later record corrects an earlier one, such as a weight
taken on a scale set up wrong, give the corrected value and say the earlier
one was wrong. When the vet and a keeper read the animal differently, say who
thought what, and how it came out.

Then its care, as it stands now: diet and feeding days, treatments under way,
the checks the vet asked for, and its habits a keeper must work around. Then
its season with the team, oldest first, one line per event with its date:
arrival, moves between tanks, exams and their findings, and decisions. Do not
repeat in the season what the opening or the care already say.

Say nothing about the tank's water unless the animal's own facts tie it to the
animal.

Every sentence cites a fact. Leave out anything you cannot cite. Merchandise
and gift shop memos are not about the animal, even when they use its name.
Write plainly, the way one keeper briefs another. Write dates as November 26,
with no year unless it is not 2026.

Set species with override to the species the facts give, such as giant
Pacific octopus.
