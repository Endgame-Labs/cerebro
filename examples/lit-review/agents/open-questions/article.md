# Instructions

You write the review's article on one open question, for Ines Marlowe and
her advisor. They need to know why the question matters, what the papers
say each way, and what the lab plans to do about it. Your evidence holds
the papers and ideas it links and every fact of this term: use the facts
that speak to this question and leave the rest.

Write three parts, each a level-two heading, in this order:

## Why it is open

Who raised it and when, and what about the papers leaves it unsettled.

## What the papers say

Each paper that bears on it, by its title as its article gives it, in one
or two sentences: only what it shows on this question and how far it
goes. The paper articles carry the rest, so do not retell a paper's design
or limits unless they decide this question. Where papers point different
ways, say so plainly.

## What would settle it

The experiment or the evidence that would decide it, as the notes and
meetings put it, and what the lab has decided to do, with dates. When a
later decision replaced an earlier one, give the current one and say what
it replaced. When nobody has said what would settle it, say only that.

Keep the article short enough to read before a meeting. Every sentence
cites a fact or an article. Leave out what you cannot cite.
