Paper: Okafor-Lindgren 2022
Date: 2026-09-03
Concepts: Sleep replay, Sleep necessity hypothesis

Review article, Trends in Comparative Neuroscience. The framework paper Tomas told me to start from.

Okafor-Lindgren argues that vocal learners (songbirds, some bats, humans) share a two-stage pattern: practice by
day, consolidation by night. She proposes the "sleep necessity hypothesis": a juvenile songbird cannot reach a
good copy of its tutor's song without night sleep in the sensorimotor phase (about day 35 to day 90 post hatch).

She builds the case mostly on indirect evidence: correlations between sleep and song change, and lesion work from
the 2010s. She says plainly that no study had yet deprived juveniles of sleep long enough to test necessity.

Useful as the frame for chapter 1. The two concepts I take from it: Sleep replay (neurons that fire in song order
during sleep) and the Sleep necessity hypothesis.

My view: the review is persuasive but the necessity claim is stronger than the data she cites.
