Paper: Brandt et al. 2025
Date: 2026-09-17
Concepts: Sleep necessity hypothesis, Morning song deterioration

Proceedings of the Nordic Society for Behavioural Biology. Brandt, Oyelaran and Sato.

They sleep-deprived 14 juvenile zebra finches by gentle handling for 5 consecutive nights (day 50 to day 55) and
compared them with 14 controls. Final tutor-song similarity at day 90 did not differ between groups (deprived
71, controls 73, not significant).

They conclude that night sleep is not necessary for song learning, directly against the Sleep necessity
hypothesis.

Two things that bother me: the deprived birds slept more on the days after deprivation ended (recovery sleep),
and 5 nights out of about 55 in the learning phase is short.

Interesting side result: the deprived birds showed no morning song deterioration on deprivation days.

My first take: the design is cleaner than people give it credit for. Tomas will not like it.
