Paper: Quill and Navarro 2024
Date: 2026-09-19
Concepts: Circadian gating

NeuroArchive preprint (not yet peer reviewed). Quill and Navarro suppressed melatonin with constant dim light in
8 juvenile zebra finches.

Result: song learning was delayed by about 10 days, though the final copy was as good. They argue song learning
is gated by the circadian clock, not by sleep as such. They call it circadian gating.

Small sample, a preprint, and constant light also disrupts sleep, so it is hard to separate the two.

Relevance: if the clock and not sleep does the work, it is an alternative to the Sleep necessity hypothesis.
