Paper: Halvorsen et al. 2023
Date: 2026-09-10
Concepts: Sleep replay, Sleep necessity hypothesis

Journal of Avian Neuroethology. Halvorsen, Pike and Abara recorded HVC neurons in 9 juvenile zebra finches
(day 45 to day 60) across whole nights.

Main result: during slow-wave sleep, HVC neurons fire in the same order as during the bird's daytime singing,
compressed about 3 times in time. They call these replay events. Replay was found in 8 of the 9 birds.

The number I will quote: birds with more replay events in a night had higher tutor-song similarity the next
morning (r = 0.61). Replay was rare in REM-like sleep.

Limits: 9 birds, correlation only, no manipulation. Similarity was scored with an older version of the
Sound Analysis tool, so scores are not directly comparable with the 2025 papers.

This is the core paper for the Sleep replay concept. It supports the Sleep necessity hypothesis only indirectly.
