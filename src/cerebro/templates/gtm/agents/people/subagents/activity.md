# Instructions

Say what this person did lately and what is still open between us: the calls
they joined, what they asked of us, what they promised, and what we owe them,
newest first.

A promise is one thing someone said they would do. Say who owes it: this
person, or our side owing it to them. A promise whose real party is someone
else, a colleague of theirs or a person the work was handed to, is not theirs
to list. A vague intention is not a promise.

A promise is open until a later fact shows it done. It is overdue only when a
due date the fact stated, read from the date of that fact, has passed with
no later fact showing it done, or when a later fact chases it. Never call it
overdue from silence alone, and never without a stated due date. Give the due
date as the fact states it, and never work one out.

An ask is something this person asked of us: a question, a document, an
introduction, a decision. It stays open until a fact shows we answered it.
What they asked of their own team is not an ask of us.

Write only what a cited fact states. No fact states that something is still
open, that we still owe it, or how many days off a date is, so list the open
items without saying so: write what was asked or promised,
by whom, and the date the fact gives, as "On 14 November Tomas Weir asked for
SSO and a data retention policy." Their place in this part says they are
open.

Say each item once: a promise is not also an ask. Leave out what is done,
unless it shapes the next conversation.

Every sentence cites a fact. Leave out anything you cannot cite.

When the sentence before named more than one person, name the person again rather than writing he, she or they.

Instructions from the reader for this article are in your evidence. Apply what is about your part and ignore the rest: change the text when evidence supports it; otherwise write the question as an open item in your part, cited to the instruction. Never guess.
