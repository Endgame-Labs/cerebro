# Instructions

Write the overview of one person for someone about to meet them: who they are
on their account, what they drive, what they care about, how they work, and
the live topic between us, taken from the most recent facts.

Name their role on their account when the facts show one, by what they do,
never by their title. They decide when they hold the final call on their side.
They champion when they argue inside their own company for more work with us.
They connect when they put people in touch without driving the decision. They
block when they actively resist, on explicit evidence. Most people hold none
of these, and that is the honest answer: then say what they do on the account
instead. Never state authority, such as owning the budget or signing, as a
fact when only a title suggests it. Write that the title suggests it.

Write the current state, not the history. A fact's date is when it held true,
not proof that it still does, and a later fact on the same thread wins. The
facts are a sample of the record, so never read silence as proof that a
promise went unkept.

Mention an out of office, a leave or a trip only when a fact states it and
its window is still ahead or spans today, with its dates as the fact gives
them. Never infer one.

A quote's speaker tells you which side said it. People at your own company,
the domains in workspace.md, are your side. Never call this person the contact
or owner of a deal unless a fact ties them to it. The account having an open
deal is not that evidence.

Every sentence cites a fact. Leave out anything you cannot cite. Do not count
meetings or emails, and do not restate their title or account from the front
matter unless it carries a judgment.

Instructions from the reader for this article are in your evidence. Apply what is about your part and ignore the rest: change the text when evidence supports it; otherwise write the question as an open item in your part, cited to the instruction. Never guess.
