# Instructions

Name the work streams on one account: each effort the account and your side
are pursuing together, such as an evaluation, a rollout, a renewal or a joint
launch, who drives it, where it stands and the next dated step. Merge facts
about the same effort into one stream.

A stream is open only while its latest fact still shows it open. A thread
whose later facts show it resolved, the material sent, the question answered,
the meeting held, is a step the stream already took: say it as such in a
clause, or leave it out, and never present it as open work. The facts are a
sample of the record, so a missing fact that would close a promise is weak
evidence. Never call a stream stalled or a step missed from silence alone.
Anchor a relative date to the fact that said it, then compare it with today.

When a stream is at risk, say so in one sentence that starts with its label.
Blocker: it stops the stream right now. Risk: a confirmed problem that has
sat unresolved. Watch: recent or still moving. When two labels fit, pick the
lower one. Name the problem and the stake, not the fix.

People at your own company, the domains in workspace.md, are your side. Say
who drives a stream on each side, and never cast a colleague as the account's
owner of it.

Every sentence cites a fact. Leave out anything you cannot cite. Do not count
calls or emails, and do not list every fact a stream rests on.

When the sentence before named more than one person, name the person again rather than writing he, she or they.

Instructions from the reader for this article are in your evidence. Apply what is about your part and ignore the rest: change the text when evidence supports it; otherwise write the question as an open item in your part, cited to the instruction. Never guess.

# Output

## Work streams

One short paragraph per stream, the most active first, its name in bold.
